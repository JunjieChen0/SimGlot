# Data terms

**Code is MIT** ([`LICENSE`](LICENSE)). **The data is not** — it is released under the terms below,
because two parts of it are not the authors' to license unconditionally.

---

## What is released, and under what terms

| Data | Where | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Frozen per-episode records** — the v1.0 main matrix (4,320 episodes), the component ablation (3,240 cells), the cross-domain run (1,200 cells) | `simglot-v1.0-final.tar.gz`, `simglot-S2-final.tar.gz` | **Released for research use and redistribution with attribution**, subject to the third-party caveat below |

| **Model-served episode transcripts** | inside the frozen records | See below |

## The two caveats, stated plainly

**1. The episode records contain outputs of third-party model services.** Agents, user simulators,
a translator and an adjudicator were all served by a commercial API. The authors hold the records
and release them for research use, but cannot grant an unconditional open licence over text
generated by another party's service. If your intended use is redistribution at scale, or
commercial reuse, treat this as an access request rather than a licence.

**2. AppWorld's protected content is not redistributed at all.** The second, independently
implemented benchmark used for the portability result ships its task data under a licence that
permits public redistribution only in encrypted form. This package therefore contains the adapter,
the applicability findings and the version fingerprint, and **no task data** — and the paper reports
no detection rate for that benchmark, because the positive control could not be reconstructed.

## What you can do without asking

- Rerun every offline analysis and reproduce every number in the paper
  (`replication/README.md` gives the three entry points; none needs network, keys or a model).
- Quote, plot and republish the derived results and the aggregate tables with attribution.
- Reuse and modify the **code** under MIT, including commercially.
- Redistribute the archives as you received them, with this file and `LICENSE` alongside.

## What you should ask about first

- Redistributing the episode records as a standalone dataset, or in a form that strips the
  attribution.
- Commercial use of the episode records (as opposed to the code, which MIT already permits).
- Anything involving AppWorld's task data — the constraint there is theirs, not the authors'.

## Why this is a separate file rather than a licence file

A single blanket licence would have had to either over-claim (granting rights over model-generated
text and third-party data the authors do not control) or under-claim (denying reuse of the analysis
products, which are plainly releasable). Splitting the two is the honest arrangement: **MIT for the
code, a stated data policy for the data.**

If a deposit platform requires one licence for the whole deposit, the data terms above are the ones
to reconcile — the code side is already unambiguous.
